# Assessment: A Bill, a Bond, and a Business Order

Treat all names, rates and cash amounts below as invented. First attempt this brief without a tool or peer. You may use a calculator or simple spreadsheet. Keep the first schedule, make one independent check, and show which step you revised. After your first attempt, optional outside critique may challenge one date, rate convention or exposure without asserting outside facts. You can use the supplied flawed memo in place of a tool. This is a proposed self-contained learning assessment, not an adopted grading policy.

## A. Household financing and investing

A household expects a necessary purchase at the end of year two. The present price of its purchase basket is $1,000, tracked in this simple exercise at 4% annual inflation. The household has $1,000 available now. It also has a distinct, committed cash receipt of $500 at the end of year one. That later money is available for the savings plan below. If not used for that plan, keep it separate and do **not** count it as part of another alternative's performance. Ignore tax and fees in all three alternatives. Unused opening cash earns zero and remains available; report it separately. None of the alternatives below is a real offer.

1. **Savings schedule:** deposit $550 today and $500 at the end of year one; the stipulated return is 5% annually and withdrawal is allowed at the due date. The remaining $450 of today's available money stays separate.
2. **Bond:** pay $950 today for a two-year instrument promising $40 after year one and $1,040 at the end of year two. For the modeled plan, reinvest the year-one coupon for one year at 5%. There is no interim sale. Report that promised scenario, not a guarantee. The remaining $50 of today's money stays separate.
3. **Diversified portfolio:** deposit all $1,000 today in a portfolio of two assets. The already combined ending values, excluding any other balances, are $1,025 in a low state or $1,245 in a high state, with equal illustrative weights. No state is a forecast or a possible loss bound.

Draw a separate timeline for each and calculate the nominal price due at year two. Compare each earmarked plan to the target before including any separate cash. Then show how much opening cash and later income each plan ties up, and how a decision would change if the household commits unused opening cash as a reserve. Compare at least one ending amount in today's purchase-basket units. Name the risk, access/contract input, and reserve decision that prevents “choose the biggest average” from being an automatic answer. There is no need to rank real securities.

## B. Working capital at the cooperative

A fictional order requires cash outlays of $2,400 on day 0 and $1,200 on day 14; the $4,800 customer payment is due day 45. The order itself has no unrestricted starting cash. The customer offers to pay $1,800 on day 10 and the remaining $3,000 on day 45. A line is genuinely available up to $2,500, with no fees or interest for this problem. Draw both ledgers before any line advances. For this comparison you can draw and repay the line instantly as needed; no other commitments and a zero cash floor apply. State the peak cash requirement and which schedule can fit. Do not count the money twice or treat the early installment as a change in project revenue.

## C. Teardown and unaided defense

A draft memo says: “The portfolio ends at the average of the two states, so it can pay a bill of that amount. The bond promises $1,080 at maturity. The cooperative's job can run without financing because its receipt is greater than its expenses.” Identify what each sentence leaves out. Use the actual case dates and one stressed input.

For the short unaided defense, receive one changed assumption from a peer or choose it at random after your first attempt: inflation becomes 5%, the first bond coupon cannot be reinvested, or the cooperative's installment is delayed until day 18. Without help, revise only the affected schedule and say which recommendation, if any, changes. Keep the other schedules and explain why they do not need to be recomputed.

## Four independent scoring dimensions (20 points)

| Dimension | Full credit (5 points) |
| --- | --- |
| Timeline and units | Correct signed amounts on their dates and consistent treatment of separate balances, nominal rates and purchasing power. |
| Calculation | Reproducible target and all three household alternatives plus both firm ledgers. The method is visible, not only the last value. |
| Risk and inference | Distinguishes states, claims and guarantees; identifies at least one observed constraint on each decision and one assumption that could overturn it. |
| Verified revision and own decision | A retained unaided attempt, an independent cross-check, clear handling of one challenge and a complete rationale after a changed input. |

Use 4 for a small mistake that does not change the decision; 3 for partially good work with one material gap; 2 for useful setup with a material unresolved error; 1 for an unsupported answer; 0 when not attempted. Be able to reproduce the key line without the original critique.

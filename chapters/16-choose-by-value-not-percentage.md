# Choose by Value, Not by Percentage Alone

A small firm has two bids for the same shop space. The smaller project earns a higher percentage, and the larger contributes more present dollars. The bids are mutually exclusive, so the percentage ranking does not tell the firm what choosing the small one costs.

## Compare choices on one basis

Consider a fictional one-year opportunity with an order-time investment and a single year-end receipt. All receipts are incremental, all other effects are ignored under the case, and both choices are feasible unless stated otherwise. Use a stipulated 10% required return for each.

| Plan | Investment today | End-year receipt | IRR | NPV at 10% |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Small | $1,000 | $1,350 | 35% | $227.27 |
| Large | $5,000 | $6,500 | 30% | $909.09 |

The **internal rate of return** is the rate making a project NPV zero. Here one period lets us divide receipts by investment and subtract one. The larger option earns a lower fraction from its own starting amount but creates more value at the shared modeled rate. Alternatively, choosing Large rather than Small costs $4,000 more today and returns an extra $5,150 at year end. The incremental NPV is about $681.82, the difference between the two project NPVs.

An investment with cash flowing out more than once after its first receipts can have no meaningful IRR or several roots; a calculator returning one rate does not remove that possibility. Payback tracks how quickly modeled cash recovers the outlay, but simple payback ignores discounting and later cash. An accounting rate of return uses accounting income over a defined book investment measure, so does not price the dated cash. A discounted profitability index compares discounted benefits to an outlay under conventional cash patterns; a per-dollar rank is not a substitute for comparing entire feasible bundles. These measures can answer descriptive questions. In the stipulated case NPV answers which exclusive choice creates more total modeled value.

**Teardown.** “Use Small because it has the better rate and invest the remaining cash at the same 35%.” No second use at 35% was supplied. Name the missing alternative and explain what would change if it genuinely existed.

**Transfer.** Revisit the problem under a $4,000 cap on initial spend with no additional committed funds. Which feasible plan has the highest modeled NPV? Then restore the original budget and lower Large's receipt to $5,700. Check whether the rank changes without needing to calculate a new IRR.

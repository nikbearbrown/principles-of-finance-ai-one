# What Does a Supplier Charge for Twenty More Days?

A supplier's price is $980 if paid in ten days and $1,000 if paid in thirty. The purchasing team calls it a 2% cost of waiting. That description is incomplete: 2% of which benchmark, for how long, and what happens to cash before the earlier deadline?

## Express the signed terms as a choice

A $1,000 fictional invoice offers “2/10, net 30,” with no other fees or conditions. The firm can pay $980 on day 10 or give up the discount and pay $1,000 on day 30. The cost of retaining the $980 for twenty more days is $20, equivalent to about 2.04% of the money retained for those twenty days. The face discount of 2% is measured against $1,000; the financing comparison uses $980 as the amount kept.

A crude simple annual extrapolation would use `(20/980) × (365/20)`. A compounded annual equivalent under hypothetical repeated comparable terms is `(1000/980)^(365/20) − 1`, about 44.59%. Neither measure proves the company can roll this transaction repeatedly. These are scale conversions for comparison; ask what a real lending or supplier agreement uses, including timing, fees and enforceable payment terms.

Compare that with an alternative lender able to supply $980 on day 10 at an all-in cost of $14 at day 30 and no other effect under the exercise. Borrow and pay early, repay $994 on day 30, versus paying $1,000 then. It is a $6 difference in this specific comparison. If no funds are actually available on day 10, the cheap theoretical route does not pay the supplier. Preserve the firm's other payments and cash rules.

## Look at the operating cycle

For a separate simplified operating cycle, take an average of 25 days held in inventory, 35 days waiting for collection, and 20 days before paying suppliers. The cash conversion cycle summary is 25 + 35 − 20 = 40 days under these conventions. Use consistent period and balance measures if estimating days from statements. Averages do not set actual settlement dates. A forward ledger can show a large supplier payment next Tuesday even if the average cash cycle is stable.

**Teardown.** “Giving the supplier an early payment always improves working capital.” It can reduce financing cost and also reduce cash on the earlier date. Name what obligation is extinguished, when, and what cheaper uses or financing are displaced.

**Transfer.** The all-in cost of the alternative 20-day funding increases to $22. Which payment path costs less by day 30? What would still have to be true about supplier terms and access to money before approving the recommendation?

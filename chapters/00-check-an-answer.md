# Check an Answer Before You Use It

A chatbot announces that a future bill can be paid with money a customer owes now. The output has a table, a currency symbol, and a concluding check mark. None of that proves the money will arrive before the bill is due. This course starts by treating a well-written answer as a draft, not evidence.

For each exercise keep four lines: **decision, inputs, calculation, check**. The first names an action and the alternatives. The second states dates, units and what the numbers represent. The third shows enough work to reproduce the result. The last asks for an independent test. A second version of the same explanation is not independent. A closing balance reconstructed from actual cash movements, a bound derived from a simpler case, or a discounted amount compounded back to its original date can be.

## An optional AI path

Attempt the problem unaided and keep that record. You may then ask an assistant to challenge one input, date or step using the fictional case only. Ask it to point to the exact source of the objection. Recalculate yourself. Report the useful change or explain why you rejected it. No account is required: a peer, the supplied erroneous answer, or a second hand calculation serves the same purpose. Do not use a chatbot to supply facts about an employer, customer, account or investment.

**Warm-up.** You need to hold 400 dollars for a bill today and can expect 700 tomorrow. You currently have 360. A response says your available cash exceeds your bill by 660. Write the date on each entry and identify today's actual gap. For a check, restate the problem with tomorrow's receipt removed. Today's answer should not change. Make the correction before using a model.

**Before moving on**, practice writing “this result is conditional on…” followed by a condition a reader could check. A financial decision can be sensible even when the input is approximate. It cannot be audited if the approximation is invisible.

# One Payment, Two Dates

You want $1,389.15 in three years. An adviser says the goal costs $1,389.15 today. Another says it costs $1,200. They are not comparing the same dated dollars. Put the target and the deposit at their dates before deciding what advice is missing.

## Move one payment along the timeline

Assume a fictional account earns an effective 5% each year for three years. It has no fees, taxes or early withdrawals. A $1,200 deposit at time zero becomes $1,260 at the end of year one, $1,323 at the end of year two and $1,389.15 at the end of year three. The second year's interest is $63, not $60. It applies to the whole amount left in the account.

In symbols, a present deposit (P) at rate (r) for (n) equal periods becomes `P × (1+r)^n`. Moving in the other direction divides by the same factor. This is not merely changing two terms in a formula. It asks the opposite question: which balance now can support a specified balance later under the stated rate assumption? To check the $1,200 result, grow it forward and see if it reaches the exact target.

## Make decisions comparable

A rate and the time count have to use the same period. If the rate is effective annual, use annual intervals; if it is effective monthly, use months. For a three-year calculation with annual rate 5%, replacing 3 by 36 without converting the rate treats each month as earning a full year's interest. If one choice is uncertain, or has fees or unacceptable access rules, a comparable after-cost return and a liquidity check may be needed as well.

**Attempt first.** Enter the same schedule in a calculator or simple sheet and label balances as time-zero, year-one, year-two, or year-three dollars. Explain why using simple interest for the entire period would give $1,380 rather than $1,389.15. Find the exact amount it misses.

**Teardown.** “A bigger annual rate means a bigger deposit is needed to hit the same fixed target.” In this model, a bigger denominator means a smaller present deposit. Test with zero interest so you do not rely on memorizing the direction.

**Transfer.** Keep the 5% return and the same $1,389.15 target but move its due date from year three to year two. What deposit is needed today? State why the comparison alone does not prove an actual bank is offering those terms.

# A Sales Forecast Is Not a Cash Forecast

The cooperative expects a busy month. The forecast ends with $1,400 cash. A customer says payment will take a week longer than expected. The manager checks that the ending balance is unchanged and declares the risk closed. The lender asked about the balance in week one, not week two.

## Give the collections their own schedule

Start a new two-week planning window with $1,200 unrestricted cash. At the end of each week the business must have at least $500. There are no other movements. Net wages and other payments are $2,200 in week one and $1,300 in week two. The base collection schedule is $1,800 and $1,900. The downside moves $400 of cleared receipts from week one to week two. The customer still owes the same total and pays it within the modeled two weeks. These are explicit cash dates, not dates of sales or of expense recognition.

| Scenario | Week 1 receipts | Week 1 closing cash | Week 2 receipts | Week 2 closing cash |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Base | 1,800 | 800 | 1,900 | 1,400 |
| Slow collections | 1,400 | 400 | 2,300 | 1,400 |

Use opening cash plus receipts less payments at each date, and carry the balance to the next date. As a check across the window, $1,200 plus total collections $3,700 minus total payments $3,500 equals $1,400 in both scenarios. The repeatable check catches many arithmetic errors, but not a breach along the path.

## Convert an assumption into a trigger

If at least $1,500 clears in week one, the balance meets $500 given the other inputs. Otherwise the company needs a date-specific remedy. A conditional choice could be to require a confirmed earlier payment or a written funding commitment before authorizing another cash use. A ratio is not equivalent evidence. A forecast format that labels orders, invoices and expected settlements as one number obscures this choice.

In this example changing one input, timing of a named receipt, is a sensitivity. A more complete downside scenario might change collections, expenditures and borrowing access together where the firm has a defensible linkage. Do not imply a probability just because the scenario is called “downside.” Do not claim all daily payments clear when the observed constraints are only weekly.

**Teardown.** “Since ending cash is identical, moving collections cannot matter.” Point to the first failing date, quantify the missing cash, and name the condition that would make the statement safe for the specified weekly rule.

**Transfer.** Under the downside, exactly $100 of week-two receipts can move back to week one at no cost. Rebuild both weeks and identify which limit is met at equality. If the arrangement instead requires a $20 fee in week one, repeat the check before calling the remedy funded.

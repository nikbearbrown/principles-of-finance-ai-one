# Trace the Money From New Claims

A firm can borrow at 6%. The plant manager uses 6% to value a project and calls that “the price of the money.” The lender's money covers only part of the firm's assets. Owners have their own required return, and a project might carry a different risk from the firm.

## Begin with the money trail

A company borrowing under a loan receives cash and accepts dated payment obligations. A company issuing new shares receives the primary issue proceeds net of its costs; a trade between existing and new holders of old shares can change who owns them without funding company operations. A privately retained profit figure does not establish the amount of spendable retained cash. Ask which entity receives cash, on which date, and what claim is exchanged for it.

Suppose the modeled company funds comparable projects at market-value target weights of 60% equity and 40% debt. Required equity return in this illustration is 10%; borrowing cost is 6%; the marginal cash-tax saving from interest is usable at an assumed 25% rate. These are stipulated, not live financing quotes or tax rules. A simple blended cost consistent with those assumptions is:

`0.60 × 10% + 0.40 × 6% × (1 − 0.25) = 7.80%`.

The equity term contributes 6 percentage points and the debt term 1.8. If the tax saving is not usable under otherwise unchanged inputs, that blend becomes 8.40%. The rate is for suitably matched operating cash flows before interest and principal. Do not subtract lender payments in those cash flows and then claim the blended rate already treats them consistently.

## Decide when the number fits

Weights should relate to the policy for the priced stream, not automatically the old book balances or one invoice's payment source. A more speculative project may require different risk inputs. Even where present value is positive, high required debt payments can cause a cash problem before projected returns arrive. Loan quotes and amounts, legal terms and actual project risk are separate verification jobs.

**Teardown.** “The firm raised debt more cheaply than equity, so replacing all equity by debt will mechanically lower every project's required return.” The example held returns fixed for one mix. Changing leverage changes who absorbs cash-flow risk and can alter what both lenders and owners charge. There is no proof of unchanged terms at a different mix.

**Transfer.** With the original mix and tax assumption, raise the borrowing rate to 8%, holding other stipulated inputs fixed for sensitivity. Compute the new blend. List one piece of evidence needed before calling either blend an appropriate rate for the shop's first overseas venture.

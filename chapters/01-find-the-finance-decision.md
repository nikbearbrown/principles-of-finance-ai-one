# Find the Finance Decision

At the community print cooperative, someone points to a $4,800 customer invoice and says, “We can take that order.” The invoice is real in the story. Its cash is not here yet. The choice is not just whether the order produces a surplus. It is whether the cooperative can carry the order from the first payment to the final collection.

## Separate three questions

An **investment** choice asks what cash the activity requires and returns relative to an alternative. A **financing** choice asks how the upfront commitment is funded and on what terms. A **risk** question asks which inputs can change and what would happen to the decision. A household choosing a savings plan faces the same distinctions in a different setting: the goal has a date, available funding has limits, and outcomes may vary. This book teaches the mechanism, not what a particular household should buy.

The fictional cooperative has no cash assigned to the job initially. It pays $2,400 for materials on day 0 and $1,200 for wages on day 14. It collects $4,800 on day 45. There are no other flows, no financing charges, and cash cannot fall below zero without outside funds. Use end-of-day balances after the listed events. Take the order or turn it down; the case gives no already-committed loan.

| Day | Net cash movement ($) | Running balance without funding ($) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 0 | −2,400 | −2,400 |
| 14 | −1,200 | −3,600 |
| 45 | +4,800 | +1,200 |

**Attempt first.** Before using the final line, find when the running balance is lowest. The case needs as much as $3,600 of temporary funding even though the project contributes $1,200 in total cash before financing cost. The negative balances are diagnostic gaps, not an instruction to pay bills from an empty account. This is a cash result, not a conclusion about when the firm earns accounting income.

## Test the hinge

Move the customer payment to day 10 with no change to the amounts. The running cash is now −2,400, then +2,400, then +1,200. The funding need drops to $2,400 and total cash contribution is unchanged. An earlier payment changes a financing question without creating additional revenue. Check the timing change against contract terms before assuming it is available.

**Teardown.** A supplied response uses the date the invoice is issued as the date it is paid. Circle the line in the case that contradicts that assumption. Correct the response using only dates and cash it can support.

**Transfer.** The customer instead pays $1,000 of the bill when ordering and the remaining $3,800 on day 45. Draw the new ledger. How much temporary funding is still needed? Name the authorization or settlement evidence you would require before booking the job.

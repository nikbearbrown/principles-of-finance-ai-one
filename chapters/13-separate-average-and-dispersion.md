# Average Return Is Not Ending Wealth

Two investments have the same average annual return in your table. One leaves less money after two periods. To diagnose the disagreement, ask whether the average describes observations, compounds wealth, or estimates risk.

## Start with a short sample you can audit

In a fictional two-year sample, A has returns of −10% and +10%. B has returns of 0% and 0%. Both arithmetic means are 0%. From $100, A falls to $90 and then grows to $99, while B remains at $100. Multiplying (0.90 × 1.10 = 0.99) tells you A's total compounded return is −1%. The constant two-period annual growth rate is the square root of 0.99 minus one, about −0.50%. It gives the same ending balance when compounded twice.

An arithmetic mean describes a typical one-period observation in this sample's arithmetic sense. It isn't the equivalent annual compound rate. Weighting and sampling matter: a sequence with extra deposits or withdrawals is not summarized by the same simple endpoint formula.

## Measure a different feature deliberately

To describe dispersion with the **sample standard deviation** convention, subtract the sample mean from each observation, square, sum, divide by (n-1), then take the square root. Expressing returns as decimals, A has sum of squared deviations 0.02. With two observations, the square root of 0.02 is about 0.1414, or 14.14 percentage points of return. B has no variation in these two rows and sample deviation 0. With the descriptive population convention for these two values A's divisor would instead be 2 and its standard deviation 10 percentage points. Document the convention instead of declaring one answer “more accurate” without stating the goal.

Two observations are fragile estimates. B's lack of observed movement does not prove future safety. Standard deviation treats high and low deviations symmetrically. It is not the biggest loss a particular holder can experience. For a bill on a specified date, size and timing of a possible shortfall may matter more than a symmetrical summary.

**Teardown.** “A returns 0% on average and can never lose more than its standard deviation.” The first clause names a sample summary, and the second invents a guaranteed bound. Describe a future observation that the model hasn't ruled out.

**Transfer.** Replace A's returns with −20% and +25%. Compute its arithmetic mean, total change from $100 and equivalent annual compound rate. Which quantity can you independently check by growing the ending value back from the opening value?

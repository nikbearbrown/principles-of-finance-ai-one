# Link the Statements Without a Plug

An analyst reads the profit figure and announces that the cooperative must finish with more cash than it started with. The closing balance is lower. Both numbers can be right. The easiest way to catch an actual error is to produce the cash result by two different paths.

## Add the rest of the company

Start a fresh reporting period with $8,000 cash and $8,000 owner equity, no debt and no other assets. Include the job's $4,800 recognized revenue, $2,400 paper expense, and $1,200 cash wage expense. This time the firm also pays $3,000 cash for equipment, recognizes $300 of depreciation, and has no other events, owner transactions or income tax in the case. At day 30 the customer has not paid; the full credit sale remains collectible under the exercise's assumptions. All costs are paid except the noncash depreciation entry. The earlier chapter's order dates otherwise still apply.

| Day 30 income statement items | Dollars |
| --- | ---: |
| Revenue | 4,800 |
| Paper and wages | −3,600 |
| Depreciation | −300 |
| **Net income** | **900** |

In this model the cash flow from operations is −3,600: no customer collection by the cutoff, but wages and paper have been paid. From net income, add back 300 for depreciation and subtract 4,800 for the increase in receivables. The result is again −3,600. There is no ending inventory from the job and no payable. The operations bridge is separate from the investing outflow of 3,000 for equipment. There are no financing flows in the period.

| Day 30 balance sheet | Dollars |
| --- | ---: |
| Cash: 8,000 − 3,600 − 3,000 | 1,400 |
| Receivables | 4,800 |
| Equipment less depreciation | 2,700 |
| **Total assets** | **8,900** |
| Liabilities | 0 |
| Equity: 8,000 + 900 | 8,900 |

The income statement explains one period's performance, the balance sheet shows positions on the measurement date, and the cash statement reconciles cash movement over that period. A separate statement of changes in equity would distinguish owner contributions and distributions from earnings. The [SEC financial statements overview](https://www.sec.gov/investor/pubs/begfinstmtguide.htm) is a reference for the broad distinctions. None of these statements alone substitutes for the next month's payment forecast.

**Teardown.** A draft cash statement lists the price of equipment as a wage payment. Total cash is unaffected by that classification error. Operating cash is understated and the investing outflow disappears. Ask which checks catch this when the bank balance still ties.

**Transfer.** Assume no intervening transactions except collection of the full $4,800 on day 45. Update both cash and receivables, then explain why equity and net income do not increase just because the deposit arrived. Separately calculate what day-30 cash would have been if $1,000 arrived before the cutoff.
